### 1. General Situation and Case Notification
The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) continues to maintain heightened national surveillance across all points of entry and health facilities. Following confirmed reports of viral hemorrhagic fever activity within the West African sub-region and the confirmation of an imported index case managed in designated isolation facilities, members of the public are advised to remain calm, vigilant, and adhere strictly to preventive measures.

### 2. Symptoms of Ebola Virus Disease (EVD)
Ebola Virus Disease is a severe, often fatal illness in humans. The incubation period—the time interval from infection with the virus to onset of symptoms—is typically between 2 and 21 days. Humans are not infectious until they develop symptoms.

Initial symptoms are sudden and include:
- High fever (temperature equal to or exceeding 37.5°C)
- Severe headache and muscle or joint aches
- Extreme weakness and lethargy
- Sore throat and difficulty swallowing

As the disease progresses, individuals may experience:
- Severe vomiting and persistent diarrhoea
- Abdominal stomach pains
- Impaired kidney and liver function
- In severe stages: unexplained internal and external bleeding, such as bleeding from the gums, nose, eyes, or blood in stool or vomit.

### 3. How the Virus Spreads (Transmission)
Ebola does not spread through airborne transmission (it is not spread through air like influenza or tuberculosis). Transmission occurs through:
- Direct contact (via broken skin or mucous membranes in the eyes, nose, or mouth) with blood, secretions, organs, or other bodily fluids (such as saliva, sweat, vomit, urine, or semen) of an infected person.
- Contact with surfaces, clothing, bedding, or medical equipment contaminated with infected fluids.
- Direct contact with the body of a deceased person who died from Ebola. Traditional funeral rituals that involve touching the body must be strictly avoided.
- Direct contact with the blood, meat, or body fluids of infected wild animals, particularly fruit bats, monkeys, and chimpanzees.

### 4. Preventive Guidelines for the Public
The NCDC urges all Nigerians and residents to practice the following:
- Hand Hygiene: Wash hands frequently and thoroughly with clean running water and soap, or use an alcohol-based hand rub with at least 60% alcohol.
- Physical Distancing: Avoid physical contact with anyone who presents with unexplained fever and bleeding.
- Food Safety: Avoid hunting, handling, or consuming wild bushmeat. Ensure all animal products are cooked thoroughly before consumption.
- Suspected Exposure: If you suspect that you or someone near you has had contact with an infected person or exhibits symptoms, isolate immediately in a separate room and alert health authorities. Do not attempt self-medication.
